## Coverage Rule

The article must not claim that one connection format automatically has better nationwide coverage.

Coverage depends on:

- underlying network;
- provider;
- plan;
- location;
- terrain;
- congestion;
- device support;
- applicable usage policies.

Any provider-level coverage claim must be verified from current evidence.

## Competitive Gaps

### Provider Bias

Many comparison pages are published by an eSIM seller or Pocket WiFi provider.

A001 must remain neutral and transparent.

### Oversimplified Recommendations

The article must not stop at:

```text
Solo = eSIM
Group = Pocket WiFi
```

It must also consider:

- group separation;
- device compatibility;
- laptop use;
- hotspot permissions;
- fair-use policies;
- total trip cost.

### Weak Coverage Claims

The article must avoid unsupported statements that one format is always better in rural Japan.

### Static Price Comparisons

Prices and plans can change.

Current provider-level claims must be verified before publication and should be structured for future updates.

## Approved Quick-Answer Logic

Choose an eSIM when:

- the phone is unlocked and eSIM-compatible;
- the traveler wants an independent connection;
- pickup, return, and an extra device are undesirable;
- the required data and hotspot use fit the plan.

Choose Pocket WiFi when:

- the phone cannot use an eSIM;
- several devices need one shared connection;
- the group will usually remain together;
- WiFi-password setup is preferred;
- the rental’s actual data and fair-use terms suit the trip.

The user must not decide from the words `unlimited` or `better coverage` alone.

The article must instruct the user to check:

- provider;
- network;
- fair-use policy;
- hotspot rules;
- device compatibility;
- total trip cost.
